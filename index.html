The ENDÛRE story

Mile 19, 5:12 a.m. The river’s still dark, and a quiet voice says stop.

Nobody’s watching. Nobody would blame you.

You keep going anyway. Slower, but still going.

The finish line was never the point. Staying in it is.

There’s a word for that, and it’s our name. ENDÛRE: Endure Athletics, out of New York.

You stay in it on the Williamsburg Bridge before sunrise, on a Hudson Valley trail at noon, along the river at midnight.

Even the best reach a point where every mile costs something.

Courtney Dauwalter calls the hardest stretch of a race the “pain cave.” She pictures herself chiseling it wider every time.

Eliud Kipchoge, two-time Olympic marathon champion, has written down every workout in a notebook since 2003, and keeps them all at home.

Tatyana McFadden won the women’s wheelchair races in Boston, London, Chicago and New York four years straight. Sixteen majors in a row.

At 82, Ted Corbitt, founding president of New York Road Runners, walked 303 miles in six days. Around a one-mile loop on Wards Island.

Named with admiration. None of these athletes endorses or is affiliated with ENDÛRE.

You won’t run their races. That point will find you anyway, on your own loop, at your own pace.

Maybe it’s mile 3 of your first 5K. Maybe it’s mile 62 at 2:14 in the morning.

Most days, you meet it alone.

So we’re starting a crew for the part that hurts.

For the ones who keep getting up.

For the ultramarathoner and the trail runner, mud to the knees. For the first-timer in Prospect Park, half grinning, half wrecked.

For the marathoner on the Queensboro at first light. For the one stretching by the East River, straight off a night shift.

And everyone on the long way back, from an injury, a bad year, a race you didn’t finish.

Every pace counts. Every comeback counts more.

Slow is fine. Stopping to breathe is fine.

We’ll wait at the top of the hill for the last one up. Miss a month, and you’ll still be in.

Next time it’s mile 19 and that voice says stop, you’ll know you’re not the only one still going.

The first run hasn’t happened yet. Be there for it.
